Case study 03

An audit score every manager can trust, and compare

Property audits ran on paper and spreadsheets, each in its own format, with no evidence and no central record. Problems surfaced weeks late and no two properties could be compared fairly. I wrote the PRD and owned the product that replaced it: one configurable platform for room, cluster and customer-experience audits, with weighted scores, live geotagged evidence and a real-time view of property quality.

Context

Three kinds of audit, no common system

Three teams audit every property. Unit Leads check room condition and readiness each month or at room turnover. Cluster Managers check operations and process adherence each month. The Customer Experience team runs surprise audits of resident experience and service quality.

Each team used its own paper form or spreadsheet, including a 151-point unit checklist. Cleanliness was one of the top three reasons residents complained, and it came up in 35% of negative reviews. Yet leadership had no single place to see which properties were slipping.

Problem

A score nobody could trust

  • Inconsistent formats. Every auditor scored differently and nothing was tracked centrally, so properties and auditors couldn't be compared.
  • No evidence. A ticked box proved nothing: no photo, no location, no time. Cleanliness complaints stayed at 4.8 per 100 residents a month.
  • Flat scoring. Every item had equal weight, so a missing fire extinguisher counted the same as a dusty shelf.
  • Late signals. Compliance failures surfaced weeks after the audit, long after the chance to fix them cheaply.
Root causeThe audit measured activity, not risk, and nobody owned what happened after it. Digitising the paper form would only have made the same mistake faster.
Discovery

What the auditors and the records told me

  • I joined auditors at 12 properties. Some "audits" were filled in from the office, which made evidence captured on site non-negotiable.
  • I reviewed 1000 past audits. 42% of failed items failed again in the next audit, because nothing escalated a failure.
  • Managers spent 6 hours a week compiling scores by hand and still couldn't compare properties.
  • Access had to follow the audit type, not just seniority. CX audits are surprise checks, so property and cluster teams must not see them.

What we deliberately left out of v1: offline audits, maintenance execution, billing and anything resident-facing. Offline mode was the most requested, but evidence integrity came first, so it moved to a later phase.

What shipped

One workflow, three audit types, seven roles

Every audit follows the same path, and only Operations Excellence can approve, reject or reopen:

  1. DraftAuditor
  2. In progressGPS + start time
  3. SubmittedLive photo · locked
  4. Approved / RejectedOps Excellence
  5. ClosedFull history kept

Three audit types

AuditPurposeConducted byFrequencyScoring
UL (Room) AuditRoom condition, cleanliness, readinessUnit LeadMonthly or on room turnoverWeighted, pass/fail threshold
CM AuditOperations and process adherenceCluster ManagerMonthly per clusterWeighted, overall rating band
CX AuditResident experience and service qualityCustomer ExperienceSurpriseWeighted, mapped to satisfaction rating
  • Create Audit: pick a property and an audit type. The type list only shows what that role is allowed to conduct. The right checklist loads, and GPS and start time are captured automatically.
  • Evidence rules: mandatory questions must be answered, and submission requires a live photo with geolocation. Submitting locks the audit for review.
  • Checklist configuration: Ops Excellence defines categories, questions (yes/no, rating, single choice, numeric), mandatory flags, weights and pass/fail thresholds per audit type. Changing a weight never rewrites historical audits.
  • Score calculation: weighted question and category scores roll up to a total, a percentage, pass/fail and a rating band.
  • Notifications: assigned, started, submitted, approved, rejected, overdue and escalation alerts via in-app, email and WhatsApp.
  • Dashboard & Reports: pending and completed counts, average score, property-wise and auditor-wise performance, trends and failed-audit alerts. Five reports (audit summary, property compliance, auditor performance, failed audits, overdue audits) export to Excel or PDF.

Rollout

Agree the weights before go-live

We piloted at 12 properties for 6 weeks. Unit Leads were trained with a 10-minute video in Hindi. Ops leadership signed off the category weights and pass thresholds before go-live, so nobody could dispute a score later.

Learnings

Two things I'd do differently

  • Scores briefly dropped after launch because the new weighting exposed real gaps. I should have warned managers it would look worse before it looked better.
  • Live photo plus GPS on submit was the right call for trust, but weak signal in some buildings blocked submissions. I'd have planned offline capture with deferred sync from the start instead of pushing it to a later phase.
